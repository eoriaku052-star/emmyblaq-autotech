Understand Every Vehicle Sensor

A practical reference for auto electricians, technicians and learners. Learn what vehicle sensors do, where they are commonly found, the symptoms of faults, and the right approach to diagnosis.

1. Engine Management Sensors

Illustrative engine-bay reference
CKP

Crankshaft Position Sensor

Function: Reports crankshaft position and speed to help the ECU control ignition and injection timing.

Location: Commonly near the crankshaft, crank pulley or flywheel housing.

Fault signs: Cranking without starting, sudden stalling, intermittent no-start or missing RPM signal.

Testing: Check scan-tool RPM while cranking, wiring and connector condition. Test the signal using the correct procedure for the sensor type.

Tip: A zero RPM reading is a clue, not proof that the sensor itself is faulty.

Illustrative engine reference
CMP

Camshaft Position Sensor

Function: Helps the ECU identify camshaft position and synchronise engine operation.

Location: Usually on or near the cylinder head or camshaft housing.

Fault signs: Long cranking, rough running, misfires, reduced performance or a cam/crank correlation code.

Testing: Inspect supply, ground and signal as specified. Compare cam and crank signals when the equipment supports it.

Tip: Check timing-chain or belt alignment when correlation faults remain.

Illustrative engine and intake reference
MAF

Mass Air Flow Sensor

Function: Measures the mass of air entering the engine so the ECU can calculate fuel delivery.

Location: Commonly fitted in the intake duct between the air filter and throttle body.

Fault signs: Poor acceleration, rough idle, excessive fuel use or air-flow-related trouble codes.

Testing: Check live data, intake leaks, wiring and the air filter. Compare readings with vehicle-specific specifications.

Tip: Do not touch the delicate sensing element or spray it with an unapproved cleaner.

Illustrative engine reference
MAP

Manifold Absolute Pressure Sensor

Function: Measures absolute pressure in the intake manifold; some systems use it to calculate engine load and boost.

Location: On or connected to the intake manifold, depending on design.

Fault signs: Poor power, unstable idle, incorrect fuelling or pressure-related fault codes.

Testing: Compare key-on engine-off pressure with local barometric pressure, then observe changes while running.

Tip: Check blocked ports, vacuum leaks and wiring before replacing the sensor.

Illustrative engine reference
TPS

Throttle Position Sensor

Function: Reports throttle-plate position to the engine control system.

Location: On the throttle body or incorporated into the electronic throttle assembly.

Fault signs: Hesitation, uneven throttle response, limp mode or throttle-position codes.

Testing: Watch live throttle data through the full operating range. Check for smooth signal changes and wiring faults.

Tip: Electronic throttle systems may use two related position signals for safety.

Illustrative vehicle reference
APP

Accelerator Pedal Position Sensor

Function: Tells the ECU how far the driver presses the accelerator pedal.

Location: Usually integrated into the accelerator pedal assembly.

Fault signs: Weak or limited acceleration, limp mode and pedal-position codes.

Testing: Compare the pedal's redundant live-data tracks and check for smooth, correlated changes.

Tip: Never assume a pedal fault from low power alone; check throttle and engine data too.

Illustrative engine reference
ECT

Engine Coolant Temperature Sensor

Function: Measures engine coolant temperature for fuelling, fan control and other ECU strategies.

Location: Commonly in a coolant passage, cylinder head, thermostat housing or coolant outlet.

Fault signs: Incorrect temperature readings, unusual fan operation, poor cold starting or temperature-related codes.

Testing: Compare cold live data with ambient temperature and check the reading as the engine warms.

Tip: Allow the engine to cool before opening any pressurised cooling-system component.

Illustrative intake reference
IAT

Intake Air Temperature Sensor

Function: Measures the temperature of incoming air so the ECU can adjust engine calculations.

Location: In the intake duct, air-flow sensor or intake manifold, depending on design.

Fault signs: Implausible intake temperature readings, poor drivability or an IAT-related code.

Testing: Compare cold readings with ambient temperature and inspect the sensor circuit against specifications.

Tip: Some vehicles combine the IAT and MAP sensors in one housing.

Illustrative engine reference
KS

Knock Sensor

Function: Detects engine vibration patterns associated with combustion knock, allowing ignition adjustment.

Location: Commonly mounted on the engine block or cylinder head.

Fault signs: Knock-sensor codes, reduced performance or the ECU using conservative ignition timing.

Testing: Check mounting, wiring and scan data; follow the manufacturer's signal test procedure.

Tip: A knock code can also result from wiring, connector or mechanical problems.

Illustrative engine reference
O2 / Lambda

Oxygen Sensor

Function: Measures oxygen in exhaust gas to support mixture control and emissions monitoring.

Location: In the exhaust, often before and after the catalytic converter.

Fault signs: Oxygen-sensor or heater codes, abnormal fuel trims and possible increased fuel consumption.

Testing: Check heater circuit, wiring, live data and exhaust leaks. Use the correct test for narrowband or wideband type.

Tip: A sensor code does not automatically mean the sensor is bad; mixture or exhaust faults can trigger it.

Illustrative engine reference
Oil Pressure

Engine Oil Pressure Sensor or Switch

Function: Reports or switches according to engine oil pressure, depending on system design.

Location: Usually installed in an oil gallery or engine block.

Fault signs: Oil-pressure warning or implausible pressure data.

Testing: Verify actual pressure using the specified mechanical gauge procedure before condemning the electrical component.

Tip: If the oil-pressure warning stays on while the engine runs, stop the engine safely and investigate promptly.

Illustrative engine reference
BARO

Barometric Pressure Sensor

Function: Measures atmospheric pressure to help the ECU compensate for altitude and air-density changes.

Location: May be inside the ECU, integrated with another sensor or separately mounted.

Fault signs: Implausible pressure data or related engine-management codes.

Testing: Compare readings with local atmospheric pressure and the manufacturer's expected values.

Tip: Do not confuse barometric pressure with intake-manifold pressure under load.

2. Fuel, Turbocharger and Exhaust Sensors

Illustrative fuel-system reference
Fuel Pressure

Fuel Pressure Sensor

Function: Reports fuel pressure in systems designed with electronic pressure monitoring.

Location: May be fitted to the fuel rail, high-pressure pump or fuel delivery system.

Fault signs: Starting problems, power loss, pressure codes or disagreement between commanded and actual pressure.

Testing: Compare requested and actual pressure using scan data and the manufacturer's safe pressure-testing procedure.

Tip: High-pressure direct-injection and diesel systems can be dangerous. Never loosen a pressurised fuel line casually.

Illustrative vehicle reference
FTP

Fuel Tank Pressure Sensor

Function: Monitors pressure or vacuum in the fuel tank for evaporative-emissions diagnostics.

Location: Often part of the tank or evaporative-emissions system.

Fault signs: EVAP-related codes or failed leak-monitor tests.

Testing: Inspect hoses, cap and wiring; interpret scan data using the correct EVAP test procedure.

Tip: A loose fuel cap or leaking hose can mimic a sensor problem.

Illustrative fuel-system reference
Fuel Composition

Fuel Composition Sensor

Function: On supported flex-fuel systems, estimates fuel alcohol content and may report fuel temperature.

Location: Usually installed in a fuel line on vehicles designed for this system.

Fault signs: Incorrect composition data or related control-system codes.

Testing: Verify scan data, wiring and the correct signal specification for that vehicle.

Tip: Not all petrol vehicles use a separate fuel-composition sensor.

Illustrative exhaust-system reference
EGT

Exhaust Gas Temperature Sensor

Function: Measures exhaust temperature to protect components and support emissions strategies.

Location: May be before or after a turbocharger, catalyst or DPF.

Fault signs: Temperature-sensor codes, restricted regeneration or power limitations on supported vehicles.

Testing: Compare cold readings and operating changes; inspect the heat-exposed wiring and connector.

Tip: Exhaust parts remain extremely hot after shutdown.

Illustrative exhaust-system reference
DPF Pressure

DPF Differential Pressure Sensor

Function: Measures pressure difference across a diesel particulate filter to help estimate restriction.

Location: Usually connected to pressure pipes or hoses before and after the filter.

Fault signs: DPF pressure codes, failed regeneration or reduced engine power.

Testing: Check hoses for splits, blockage or heat damage; compare pressure readings under specified conditions.

Tip: A restricted filter, damaged hoses or exhaust leaks can all affect readings.

Illustrative exhaust-system reference
NOx

Nitrogen Oxide Sensor

Function: Measures NOx levels in supported emissions systems, often around SCR after-treatment.

Location: Fitted into the exhaust system at specified positions.

Fault signs: NOx or SCR codes, emissions warnings or restricted operation on some vehicles.

Testing: Check module communication, supply, wiring and live data using vehicle-specific diagnostic instructions.

Tip: Some NOx sensors have integrated electronic control modules.

Illustrative engine reference
Boost

Turbo Boost Pressure Sensor

Function: Measures intake charge pressure on turbocharged engines.

Location: Often in the intake pipe, intercooler outlet or manifold; some systems use a MAP sensor for this role.

Fault signs: Underboost or overboost codes, reduced power or limp mode.

Testing: Compare commanded and actual boost and inspect hoses, intercooler, control actuator and wiring.

Tip: Underboost does not automatically mean the sensor is faulty.

3. Transmission, ABS and Chassis Sensors

Illustrative vehicle reference
ABS / WSS

Wheel Speed Sensor

Function: Reports individual wheel speed to ABS, stability control and related systems.

Location: Near each wheel hub, bearing or tone ring.

Fault signs: ABS warning, traction-control warning or inconsistent wheel-speed data.

Testing: Compare all wheel speeds on the scan tool and inspect sensor wiring, air gap and encoder/tone ring.

Tip: Some magnetic encoder rings are built into the wheel bearing.

Illustrative vehicle reference
VSS

Vehicle Speed Sensor

Function: Supplies vehicle-speed information to the modules that need it.

Location: Depends on design; it may use transmission output speed or wheel-speed data.

Fault signs: Incorrect speed display or speed-related codes and shifting issues on some vehicles.

Testing: Compare speedometer and scan-tool data; verify the vehicle's actual sensor arrangement.

Tip: Many modern vehicles calculate vehicle speed from ABS wheel-speed signals.

Illustrative drivetrain reference
ISS / OSS

Transmission Input and Output Speed Sensors

Function: Measure rotational speed at specified points to help the transmission controller manage shifts and ratios.

Location: On or within the transmission, depending on the design.

Fault signs: Harsh shifting, incorrect gear-ratio codes, limp mode or speed-signal faults.

Testing: Compare input/output speed data and inspect the circuits using the transmission-specific procedure.

Tip: Check fluid condition and mechanical faults alongside electrical diagnosis.

Illustrative vehicle reference
TRS

Transmission Range Sensor

Function: Reports the selected transmission range, such as Park, Reverse, Neutral or Drive.

Location: Often at the transmission selector shaft or integrated into the range assembly.

Fault signs: Incorrect gear indication, no-start in Park/Neutral or range-related codes.

Testing: Compare selector position with scan data and inspect adjustment, linkage and wiring.

Tip: Follow the correct adjustment procedure before replacing the assembly.

Illustrative vehicle reference
SAS

Steering Angle Sensor

Function: Measures steering-wheel angle for stability control and other driver-assistance functions.

Location: Often in or near the steering column or clock-spring assembly.

Fault signs: Stability-control warnings or implausible steering-angle data.

Testing: Compare live angle with actual steering position and check calibration requirements.

Tip: Wheel alignment or steering work may require calibration on some vehicles.

Illustrative vehicle reference
Yaw / G

Yaw Rate and Lateral Acceleration Sensors

Function: Measure vehicle rotation and sideways acceleration for stability-control calculations.

Location: Often mounted near the vehicle centre, sometimes within a combined sensor unit.

Fault signs: Stability-system warnings or implausible dynamic data.

Testing: Check live readings at rest, wiring, mounting and any required zero-point calibration.

Tip: Use the manufacturer's calibration procedure after relevant repairs.

4. Air Conditioning and Body Sensors

Illustrative automotive reference
A/C Pressure

Refrigerant Pressure Sensor

Function: Reports refrigerant-system pressure so the control system can manage compressor operation and protection.

Location: Commonly installed on a refrigerant line or pressure connection.

Fault signs: AC compressor may not engage, pressure data may be implausible or an AC fault may be stored.

Testing: Compare scan data with proper manifold-gauge readings and inspect wiring and connector condition.

Tip: Incorrect refrigerant charge, leaks or cooling-fan problems can also cause AC faults.

Illustrative AC-system reference
Evaporator Temp

Evaporator Temperature Sensor

Function: Monitors evaporator temperature to help control cooling and prevent excessive icing.

Location: Usually fitted in or near the evaporator assembly inside the HVAC housing.

Fault signs: Intermittent cooling, evaporator icing or implausible temperature data.

Testing: Compare sensor readings with actual temperature and check resistance or signal using the correct specification.

Tip: Restricted airflow and refrigerant faults can produce similar symptoms.

Illustrative vehicle interior
Cabin Temp

Cabin Temperature Sensor

Function: Measures cabin air temperature for automatic climate-control systems.

Location: Often on the dashboard or HVAC panel, sometimes with a small aspirator fan.

Fault signs: Automatic AC temperature control may feel inaccurate or respond poorly.

Testing: Compare scan data with a reliable cabin thermometer and inspect any sensor fan or air passage.

Tip: Dust or a blocked aspirator can affect readings.

Illustrative vehicle exterior
Ambient Temp

Outside Air Temperature Sensor

Function: Measures outside temperature for the dashboard display and, where fitted, climate-control strategies.

Location: Often behind the front grille or near the front bumper.

Fault signs: Incorrect outside temperature display or related climate-control symptoms.

Testing: Compare the reading after the vehicle has settled to ambient conditions and inspect the exposed wiring.

Tip: Heat from the engine or road surface can temporarily affect readings.

Illustrative vehicle interior
Sunload

Sunload Sensor

Function: Detects sunlight intensity to help automatic climate control adjust cooling.

Location: Commonly on top of the dashboard near the windscreen.

Fault signs: Automatic climate control may not compensate properly for direct sunlight.

Testing: Monitor live data and follow the manufacturer's method for checking sensor response.

Tip: Do not cover the sensor during climate-control testing.

Illustrative vehicle reference
Rain

Rain Sensor

Function: Detects water on the windscreen to control automatic wipers on supported vehicles.

Location: Usually attached to the inside of the windscreen near the rear-view mirror.

Fault signs: Automatic wipers may not respond correctly or may operate unexpectedly.

Testing: Check the sensor mounting, optical coupling pad, wiring and diagnostic data.

Tip: Windscreen replacement can affect sensor operation if installation is incorrect.

Illustrative automotive reference
Fuel Level

Fuel Level Sender

Function: Measures fuel quantity in the tank for the dashboard gauge and range calculations.

Location: Commonly part of the in-tank fuel-pump or sender assembly.

Fault signs: Gauge stuck, inaccurate fuel indication or a reading that changes unexpectedly.

Testing: Follow the service procedure to test sender resistance or signal and inspect the instrument circuit.

Tip: Some tanks use more than one level sender.

5. Safety and Driver-Assistance Sensors

Illustrative vehicle reference
TPMS

Tyre Pressure Monitoring Sensor

Function: Monitors tyre pressure directly or uses wheel-speed information in indirect systems.

Location: Direct sensors are often attached to the wheel valve or inside the wheel; indirect systems use ABS data.

Fault signs: Tyre-pressure warning, missing sensor data or a system malfunction warning.

Testing: Identify whether the vehicle uses direct or indirect TPMS; check pressures, sensor IDs and relearn requirements.

Tip: A TPMS warning can mean low tyre pressure, not necessarily a failed sensor.

Illustrative vehicle exterior
Ultrasonic

Parking Distance Sensor

Function: Uses ultrasonic signals to estimate the distance to nearby objects.

Location: Commonly mounted in the front or rear bumper.

Fault signs: Continuous warning tone, a sensor-system message or one parking sensor not responding.

Testing: Inspect for dirt, paint damage, poor fitment and wiring faults; use system diagnostics where available.

Tip: Confirm the faulty sensor before removing the bumper.

Illustrative vehicle reference
BSM

Blind-Spot Monitoring Radar

Function: Detects vehicles in adjacent areas to support blind-spot warnings.

Location: Often behind the rear bumper corners or side body panels.

Fault signs: Blind-spot warnings unavailable or system malfunction messages.

Testing: Check module communication, mounting, obstruction and calibration requirements.

Tip: Bumper repairs can affect radar alignment; follow approved calibration procedures.

Illustrative vehicle reference
Forward Radar

Forward-Collision Radar

Function: Detects objects ahead for supported adaptive cruise-control and collision-warning systems.

Location: Often behind the grille or front emblem.

Fault signs: Driver-assistance warnings or features temporarily unavailable.

Testing: Check obstruction, mounting, fault codes and calibration requirements.

Tip: Do not adjust radar mounting by guesswork.

Illustrative vehicle reference
ADAS Camera

Forward-Facing Driver-Assistance Camera

Function: Interprets road markings, vehicles and other visual information for supported assistance features.

Location: Commonly near the top of the windscreen.

Fault signs: Lane-assistance or collision-warning features may become unavailable.

Testing: Inspect the windscreen area, module data and camera calibration status.

Tip: Windscreen replacement may require camera calibration.

Illustrative vehicle reference
SRS

Airbag Impact Sensor

Function: Provides crash-deceleration information to the supplemental restraint system.

Location: Depending on design, sensors may be at the front, sides or within restraint-system modules.

Fault signs: Airbag/SRS warning light or stored restraint-system fault codes.

Testing: Use the approved SRS diagnostic procedure and inspect only as directed by the service manual.

Tip: Airbag circuits are safety-critical. Do not probe deployment circuits with an ordinary test lamp or ohmmeter.

6. Hybrid and Electric Vehicle Sensors

Illustrative electric-vehicle reference
Battery Temp

High-Voltage Battery Temperature Sensor

Function: Monitors battery temperature so the control system can manage charging, power and thermal protection.

Location: Integrated into or placed around the high-voltage battery pack.

Fault signs: Battery temperature codes, reduced charging or power limitations.

Testing: Review battery-management live data and follow the manufacturer's high-voltage safety procedure.

Tip: High-voltage battery work requires suitable training, equipment and isolation procedures.

Illustrative electric-vehicle reference
Battery Current

High-Voltage Battery Current Sensor

Function: Measures current flowing into or out of the battery to support battery-management calculations.

Location: Often integrated into the battery pack or high-voltage power electronics.

Fault signs: Current measurement codes or inconsistent battery-management data.

Testing: Compare live data with the manufacturer's diagnostic checks and approved equipment.

Tip: Never access exposed high-voltage components without the required training and isolation procedure.

Illustrative electric-vehicle reference
Cell Voltage

Battery Cell Voltage Monitoring

Function: Measures individual cell groups or modules to help monitor battery balance and operating limits.

Location: Inside the battery pack, connected to battery-monitoring electronics.

Fault signs: Cell-voltage imbalance codes, battery warnings or reduced available power.

Testing: Review manufacturer diagnostic data; battery-pack access is for appropriately trained technicians.

Tip: A cell imbalance can have several causes and needs proper battery-level diagnosis.

Illustrative electric-vehicle reference
Isolation Monitor

High-Voltage Isolation Monitoring

Function: Monitors electrical isolation between high-voltage circuits and the vehicle chassis.

Location: Commonly part of the battery or high-voltage control system.

Fault signs: Isolation warnings, high-voltage system shutdown or related diagnostic codes.

Testing: Follow the exact manufacturer's isolation-fault procedure using approved equipment.

Tip: Treat an isolation warning as a serious safety issue. Do not bypass it.

How to Diagnose a Sensor Fault Properly

A fault code is the starting point of diagnosis, not the final answer.

Recommended Diagnostic Workflow

  1. Confirm the complaint: Ask when the fault happens and whether it is constant or intermittent.
  2. Scan all relevant modules: Record diagnostic trouble codes and freeze-frame data before clearing anything.
  3. Check the basics: Inspect connectors, damaged wires, corrosion, fuses, grounds and power supply.
  4. Read live data: Check whether the sensor reading makes sense under the current conditions.
  5. Test the circuit: Use the correct wiring diagram, meter settings and test procedure for that sensor.
  6. Check related components: Leaks, mechanical timing, blocked passages and damaged reluctor rings can mimic sensor faults.
  7. Repair and verify: Recheck live data, clear codes only when appropriate and confirm the original complaint has been resolved.

Common Sensor Symptoms: Quick Reference

These symptoms have multiple possible causes. Always confirm with testing.

Symptom Sensors or systems to investigate Other checks
Engine cranks but will not start Crankshaft, camshaft, fuel pressure Battery voltage, timing, spark, injector command and fuel supply
Rough idle MAF, MAP, ECT, TPS Vacuum leaks, ignition faults, compression and fuel trims
Weak acceleration MAF, MAP/boost, APP, fuel pressure Air leaks, exhaust restriction, fuel delivery and limp mode
ABS warning light Wheel-speed sensors Wiring, wheel bearing encoder, tone ring and ABS module
AC compressor will not engage Refrigerant pressure, evaporator temperature where fitted Refrigerant charge, compressor control, fuses, fan and climate-control requests
Incorrect fuel gauge Fuel-level sender Sender wiring, instrument cluster and fuel-pump assembly
Transmission shifts incorrectly Input/output speed, range sensor Transmission fluid, mechanical condition, wiring and control-module data
Safety reminder: Do not disconnect or test airbag circuits, high-pressure fuel systems, or high-voltage hybrid/EV components without the proper training and manufacturer-approved safety procedure.
